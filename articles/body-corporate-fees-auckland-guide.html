Decoding the Fine Print: A Buyer's Guide to Body Corporate Fees in Auckland

Buying Advice By Eric Wu 2026-04-15 4 Min Read

When buying a unit title property in East Auckland, a low body corporate fee is not automatically good news — it is often the first sign that the complex is underfunding its future repairs, and that the shortfall will arrive as a special levy with your name on it. Reading the financials properly is what separates a well-run complex from an expensive one.

What the Fees Actually Cover

  • Building insurance for the whole complex — you insure only your internal contents.
  • Exterior maintenance: painting, roof repairs, cladding.
  • Common areas: driveways, landscaping, shared lighting, fencing.
  • Rubbish collection and administrative management.

Viewed that way, the levy is not an extra cost on top of home ownership — it is the same maintenance a standalone homeowner pays, collected in advance and spread evenly.

The Long-Term Maintenance Plan Is the Real Document

Every unit title body corporate must maintain a long-term maintenance plan (LTMP) covering major future works. The question that matters is whether the fund actually holds enough to deliver it. A complex charging low levies while facing a roof replacement or a full repaint in three years is quietly accumulating a liability that transfers to whoever owns the unit when the invoice lands.

What to Read Before You Offer

  • The pre-contract disclosure statement and, if you can obtain it, the pre-settlement disclosure.
  • The last two years of AGM minutes. Disputes, deferred works and looming projects show up here long before they appear in the levy.
  • The LTMP and the long-term maintenance fund balance, side by side. Plan without funding is the warning sign.
  • Any history of special levies, and whether the same issue is likely to recur.

Why This Matters More in East Auckland

As the area intensifies, an increasing share of Flat Bush and East Tāmaki stock is unit-titled townhouses.
