Master the Room: Practical Strategy for Buying and Selling at Auction

Buying Advice By Eric Wu 2026-07-22 5 Min Read

Auctions are won before auction day. Because an auction bid is unconditional, everything that would normally be a condition — finance, building report, LIM, legal review — has to be finished before you raise your hand. In East Auckland, where auction remains a common method for family homes, that preparation is the whole game.

For Buyers

  • Get genuinely finance-approved, not pre-approved in principle, and confirm your lender is comfortable with this specific property. A valuation condition you cannot use is not a safety net.
  • Set your limit in writing before you arrive, and decide it with the person who shares the mortgage. The room is designed to create urgency; a number written down beforehand is what protects you from it.
  • Bid clearly and where the auctioneer can see you. Confident, prompt bidding does apply pressure. Long hesitation tells the room you are near your ceiling.

On Pre-Auction Offers

A strong pre-auction offer can bring the auction forward and put you in front of buyers who have not finished their due diligence. It is a legitimate strategy — but be clear-eyed: it also alerts the market that the property has serious interest, and if the auction proceeds you have shown your hand. It works best when your offer is strong enough to be genuinely compelling, not as a probe.

For Sellers

Auction creates a deadline, and deadlines create competition. But an auction only works if the campaign has done its job first: three weeks of qualifying buyers, checking their readiness, and managing price expectations so the room contains people who can actually bid. An auction with two unprepared parties in the room is worse than a well-run private treaty campaign.

It also is not right for every property. Where a large share of likely buyers need conditions, auction narrows the field at the worst moment — we set out the trade-offs in our guide to auction versus tender.

What the Reserve Does

The reserve is the confidential minimum you will accept. Below it the property is not "on the market" and the auctioneer may make a vendor bid to move things along — a normal and regulated part of the process that we explain in what a vendor bid actually means.
